# Blueprint: the most realistic 3D route

How Tour du Salon builds the road from the saddle, and how it gets as close to the real ride as open data allows.
This is the plan of record. It draws on the research report
`reports/OSM data for realistic 3D cycling.md` (14 October 2026, all figures sourced there) and on what building phases 0–2 taught.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Measured where the data exists, generated by rule where it does not.**
   Rich OpenStreetMap tags are rare. Fewer than 3 % of buildings in the Netherlands or Turkey have `building:levels`, and about 1 % have `height` or `roof:shape`. On the rider's own roads it is worse: Maltepe has heights on 0.1 % of buildings, Oude IJsselstreek has roof shapes on 0.6 %.
   So the look is decided by **defaults keyed on tags that nearly every object has**, such as building type, highway class, `lit`, `surface`, `electrified` and pylon `design`. Rarer tags override those defaults where they exist.
2. **Deterministic.** Every procedural choice is seeded by the OSM id: a building's height nudge, its window pattern, a tree's species and offset. The same route looks the same on every ride.
3. **The GPX is the rider's line.** The rider rides the middle of the rightmost lane (the rider's decision). The road is built around that line from the matched OSM way. The camera never sits on a computed centreline.
4. **The ride never waits for data.** The road appears at once on flat ground. Terrain, then map features, are swapped in as they arrive.
5. **Offline-first.** Everything a route needs is downloaded once into a *route pack* on the rider's disk (section 6). A route that has a pack rides without a network, even after an app update changes what is fetched.
6. **The road always wins.** Ground, buildings and other roads are fitted to the ridden road, never the other way round. The GPX elevation is what the trainer feels, so it is what the eye sees.

## 2. Pipeline

```
GPX route
  └─ corridor: ±250 m (buildings, roads, furniture), ±1.2 km (land, water), ±1.5 km (coast)
       └─ sources ──► route pack on disk  (data/terrain/, data/osm/, later data/nl/…)
                         └─ scene build, pure Rust, off the main thread   (src/data/)
                              course3d   ground grid fitted to the road, camera
                              terrain    elevation tiles → heights
                              osm        Overpass answer → features
                              scenery    land use, sea, buildings
                              roadway    ridden road, other roads, levels, structures
                         └─ GL view, GTK main thread   (src/ui/widgets/course_view.rs)
```

* **Scene build budget.** Under 1.5 s in release for a 111 km route: 0.4 s for Maltepe and 1.3 s for Morning Ride in phase 1. Every new layer is measured against this.
* **Everything in `src/data/` is pure and unit-tested.** The view does no logic beyond uploading meshes and moving the camera.
* **The world is rendered, not themed.** World colours are fixed constants (CLAUDE.md §1.6).

## 3. The layers

**Status key:** ✅ built · 🔜 next · ⏳ later. NL is the Achterhoek, IST is Istanbul.

### Ground and water

**Terrain** ✅ (phase 1)
- **Measured:** AWS Terrarium tiles, zoom 13, ±1.3 km.
- **NL:** ⏳ AHN DTM at 0.5 m (CC0). The current tiles are 30 m SRTM upsampled.
- **IST:** ⏳ Copernicus GLO-30, masked where it includes rooftops.
- **Fallback:** flat ground at road level.

**Sea** ✅ (phase 2)
- **Measured:** the `natural=coastline` side test.
- **NL and IST:** OSM. The tiles store water as 0 m by design.
- **Fallback:** raw elevation below −2 m.

**Land use and land cover** ✅ (phase 2)
- **Measured:** OSM `landuse`/`natural`/`leisure` areas.
- **NL:** ⏳ BGT vegetation polygons (CC0).
- **IST:** ⏳ ESA WorldCover 10 m as a fill.
- **Fallback:** default grass.

**Rivers and ditches** ⏳
- **Measured:** OSM `natural=water`+`water=*`, and `waterway=*` lines given widths by class.
- **NL:** BGT water. OSM has 0 ditches in a test box where BGT has 27 per 1.5 km².
- **Fallback:** default widths per waterway class.

### The road

**Ridden road** ✅ (phase 2)
- **Measured:** the GPX matched to an OSM way, giving lanes, one-way, width, edge lines and paint.
- **Fallback:** a two-lane, two-way road with the rider in the right lane.

**Road cross-section** 🔜
- **Measured:** `surface`, `sidewalk`, `cycleway`, `lit`, `turn:lanes`, `crossing:markings`.
- **NL:** ⏳ BGT carriageway, cycle-path and footway polygons.
- **Fallback:** class defaults, following osm2lanes rules: NL secondary roads get a red cycle lane, residential streets get brick paving.

**Other roads** ✅
- **Measured:** OSM ways laid on the ground.

**Over- and underpasses, tunnels** ✅
- **Measured:** `layer`, `bridge`, `tunnel` (`layer` is on 94–99 % of bridges).
- **Generated:** where the route crosses itself, the upper pass becomes a bridge.

### Buildings

**Footprints** ✅
- **Measured:** OSM buildings within ±250 m.
- **NL:** OSM is complete here: 98.6 % of buildings carry `ref:bag`.
- **IST:** ⏳ Overture (ODbL), which adds Microsoft footprints.

**Heights** ✅
- **Measured:** `height`, else `building:levels` × 3 m.
- **NL:** ⏳ 3D BAG `b3_h_70p` (CC BY 4.0).
- **Fallback:** a type default ± one storey, seeded by OSM id.

**Roofs** ✅ (phase 3)
- **Measured:** `roof:shape`, `roof:colour`, `roof:height` where tagged.
- **NL:** ⏳ 3D BAG `b3_dak_type`, then the LoD2.2 roof models.
- **Fallback:** by building type and region (section 4).

**Façades** ✅ windows
- **Measured:** `building:colour`/`building:material` where tagged.
- **Generated:** shop fronts from `shop=*`, and storeys and bays drawn in the shader.

**Landmarks** 🔜
- **Measured:** `building=mosque`, `church`, `man_made=tower`/`windmill`, tower `height` (on 82 % of NL towers).
- **Generated:** domes and minarets for IST mosques (only 12 minarets are mapped against 246 mosques), and spires for churches.

### Nature

**Trees, rows and hedges** 🔜
- **Measured:** `natural=tree`, `tree_row`, `barrier=hedge`, and wood/forest areas.
- **NL:** ⏳ AHN canopy height (surface model minus terrain model) for roadside trees.
- **IST:** ⏳ WorldCover tree class.
- **Generated:** rows every 6–10 m, forest edges near the road, seeded by OSM id.

### Furniture and infrastructure

**Street furniture** 🔜
- **Measured:** lamps, benches, bollards, signals, crossings and bus stops.
- **NL:** OSM is rich here (120k lamps). ⏳ BGT light masts where municipalities supply them.
- **IST:** sparse (35 lamps in Kadıköy+Maltepe).
- **Generated:** lamps every 30–40 m on `lit=yes` and main roads.

**Rail** ⏳
- **Measured:** `railway=*` with gauge and `electrified` (99 % tagged).
- **Generated:** catenary from `electrified=contact_line`, since masts are almost unmapped.

**Power and wind** ⏳
- **Measured:** `power=line/tower` with `design` (on 83 % of NL towers), and `generator:source=wind`.
- **Generated:** heights by voltage and by rated power.

**Fences, walls and guard rails** ⏳
- **Measured:** `barrier=*`.
- **Generated:** default heights; guard rails along motorways and bridges.

**Signs** ⏳
- **Measured:** NL town-limit signs (15k), `traffic_sign` on cycle paths (63 %), street names.

### Sky

**Sky and light** ✅
- **Generated:** a gradient sky, distance haze and a fixed sun. Later: time-of-day.

## 4. Rules that carry the look

These defaults are where most of the realism comes from. Each is a small pure function with tests in `src/data/`.

* **Building height.** `height` → `building:levels`×3 → a type default: apartments 6 storeys, houses 2, sheds 1, an untagged `yes` 3. Every default gets a ±1 storey nudge seeded by id. ✅
  - In the Netherlands the guess is regional (`roofs::regional_walls`): an untagged building is a house of 1–3 storeys (mostly 2), or a barn (one 4.5 m storey) when its footprint is 250 m² or more. Farm buildings get one storey. ✅
  - Why: almost half of Montferland's 10,907 buildings have no type. A world-wide 3 storeys turned the Achterhoek into a town of flat-roofed blocks.
* **Roofs** ✅ (`src/data/roofs.rs`). A tagged height includes the roof, so wall height = height − roof height; a guessed height is the walls, with the roof on top. A pitched roof sits on the footprint's best-fit rectangle, so a footprint that fills less than 82 % of it (an L, a courtyard) is roofed flat.

  | Building | Region | Roof |
  |---|---|---|
  | `house`, `detached`, `farm`, `barn`, small `yes` (≤ 2 storeys) | NL | Gabled along the long axis, dark grey/black tiles, ridge 4–5 m |
  | Small `yes`, `house` | IST | Hipped, terracotta `#a1634f` (the colour already tagged on 18k Turkish roofs) |
  | ≥ 4 storeys, `apartments`, `commercial` | both | Flat, grey, with a parapet |
  | Tagged `roof:shape` | both | As tagged. Hipped = the ridge pulled in by ⅓ (OSM2World's shortcut); a straight skeleton comes later |

* **Region.** The country comes from the route's location (a bounding box for NL and TR is enough), never from a guess per building.
* **Lanes.** Lanes come from the tag where it exists, else a class default: trunk one-way 3, primary two-way 4, residential 2, service 1, link 1. Match pooling votes by **road shape**, never by way id (one boulevard is many ways). Slip roads carry a matching penalty. ✅
* **Lamps.** On roads tagged `lit=yes`, and on main roads in Istanbul whatever the tag says, generate lamps every 35 m on the kerb side. Real lamp nodes win within 15 m of a generated one.
* **Trees.** Real `natural=tree` nodes first. Then `tree_row` lines are filled at 8 m ± 2 m. Then forest polygons get points within 60 m of the road at roughly a 6 m spacing. Species follows `leaf_type`, else the region: NL broadleaf, IST mixed with plane and pine.
* **Never synthesise:**
  - Poles for rural Dutch medium-voltage power: those lines are underground.
  - Extra rails from `tracks`: OpenRailwayMap draws one way per track.
  - Sidewalks where a separate sidewalk way exists.

**Skip:**
- **Mapillary:** the licence for its detections is unclear.
- **Google Open Buildings:** no Turkey or Europe coverage.
- **EUBUCCO:** no Turkey; duplicates 3D BAG.
- **İBB open data:** thin and unlicensed.
- **Small Woody Features:** needs a login; unvalidated.
- **Geofabrik country extracts:** 1.3 GB for NL and 620 MB for TR per user.
- **Vector tiles:** they drop roof and level tags.

### Overpass etiquette (applies today)

* overpass-api.de allows **fewer than 100 queries and about 10 MB a day across all of an app's users**. One route is half a day's budget. Its 504s are deliberate load-shedding.
* Requests carry a User-Agent, run one at a time, and back off 30 s after a 429.
* **A cached route is never queried again** (section 6).
* **Server order is unresolved.**
  - overpass.kumi.systems answered when overpass-api.de failed on 14 October 2026.
  - Research says it now runs as overpass.private.coffee, which asks to be told in advance about large-scale use. That mirror also served data months old (May–July 2026).
  - Measure all three before changing the order. Notify private.coffee's operator if it becomes the primary server.
* **Privacy.** A route query sends the route's corridor and the rider's IP. The privacy note must name the servers. A later improvement could snap queries to tile-aligned boxes.

## 6. Offline: route packs

A route is **available offline** once its pack is on disk. A pack holds:

* **Elevation tiles:** `<data>/terrain/13/x/y.png`, shared between routes.
* **The map answer:** `<data>/osm/route-<route-key>.json`, keyed by the **route**, not by the query text. A changed query, such as a new feature type, no longer orphans an offline route.
* **Later:** `<data>/nl/…` for 3D BAG, AHN and BGT pieces, keyed by tile.

Rules:

1. **Prepare on import.** Adding a route downloads its pack in the background. The route library shows each route's state (*ready offline*, *downloading*, *not downloaded*) and offers *Download for offline* and *Retry*.
2. **Never block, never re-query.** Riding uses whatever is in the pack. A pack is only re-fetched when it predates the current query version **and** the network is up. The old answer stays in use until the new one has been fully stored and has parsed.
3. **Stale beats nothing.** With no network, an outdated pack is used as it is. The view never falls back to flat ground because an app update changed the query.
4. **Polite batch.** Packs for several routes download one at a time, never in parallel (the Overpass etiquette in section 5).
5. **Atomic.** Every file is written to `*.part` and renamed into place, so a half-written file is never read.
6. **Visible and removable.** The rider can see how much space packs take, and delete them. Packs never leave the rider's machine: a private cache is not "Public Use" under ODbL.

## 8. How every layer is verified

* **Unit tests that try to break it.** Values on the boundary, hostile input (proptest on every parser), and a deliberate mutation of the code under test that must turn the test red before it counts.
* **Offscreen renders.** `render_course_offscreen` draws the real view through EGL into a PNG. Pass `CYCLE_3D_ES=1` to render under GLES like GTK, and `CYCLE_3D_GTK_STATE=1` to reproduce GTK's leftover depth state. Always run it with `--release`.
* **Real routes.** Every layer is rendered at known places: the Maltepe start, the boulevard at 9 km, the overpass at 1 km, the interchange at 18.9 km, and the Achterhoek woods. It is also timed on the 111 km Morning Ride.
* **Desk build with a fake rider.** `CYCLE_FAKE_RIDER=200 cargo run` (debug only) rides itself in SIM.
* **The rider looks before it is committed.** The flatpak goes on the trainer for anything that moves.

## 9. Traps already paid for

* GTK leaves its GL state behind: the depth test on and depth cleared to 0. The view sets everything it uses every frame.
* The scene build must stay exact but cheap: nearest-road lookups use per-bucket candidate lists. The first version took 69–137 s in debug and showed only sky.
* Lighting normals must face the viewer, not up, or walls come out speckled.
* Wall coordinates must be carried in the vertices. Deriving them from world positions gave ragged windows.
* Dashes are cut by distance, not by GPX segments. The GPS spacing equals the dash period, which turned dashes into solid lines.
* Overpass can answer 200 with a `remark: runtime error` and partial data. Such an answer is refused and never cached.
* A route crossing itself buries its lower pass unless the ground is clamped to the lower road and the upper pass becomes a bridge.
* Geometry builders must create vertices only for what they draw. Building them for the whole route made thousands of unused vertices per structure.
